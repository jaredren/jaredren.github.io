TraceQual: An AI-Disclosure Tool for Qualitative Analysis

Independent project · Apr 2026 – Jun 2026 · github.com/jaredren/tracequal

A Python tool on the Anthropic Messages API that reads a researcher-AI chat transcript from a qualitative coding session and produces a structured decision matrix: one row per analytic decision, recording what the AI proposed, what the researcher did with it, and how confident that record is. Versioned schema grounded in the qualitative-methods literature, a rule-derived confidence model, a four-state validation harness, and a Streamlit deployment path. Meant for a methods appendix or audit trail; it never touches participant data.

Participatory Mapping of Food-Access Program Awareness at Neighborhood Farmers Markets

Research Volunteer, supervised by Dr. Elin Björling, University of Washington · 2026

Community-based participatory design with the Neighborhood Farmers Markets (U District). We co-designed a “map your route” activity and sticker cultural probes to surface how market-goers move through the market and how aware they are of food-access currency programs (EBT, SNAP Market Match, Fresh Bucks), and collected and analyzed 26 participant worksheets. The method doubled as a finding: a low-burden, ecologically valid way to capture in-the-moment route and experience data, packaged as a handoff blueprint that market staff can run themselves.

Anchor: Usability Study of Senior-Focused Puzzles

Co-lead, 4-person team (HCDE 517), University of Washington · Jan 2026 – Mar 2026

A usability study for Anchor, a company selling low-piece-count jigsaw puzzles designed for seniors and people facing cognitive challenges. We ran a cognitive walkthrough, a heuristic review, and moderated end-to-end testing (physical box to website to purchase) with seven caregiver participants, then delivered a severity- and criticality-ranked set of nine findings and recommendations to the sponsor.
