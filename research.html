AI-Mediated Communication

Examining Students’ Use of LLMs for Interpersonal Communication in Academic Settings [Ongoing]

Lead Researcher, University of Washington · Jan 2026 – present

An IRB-approved, two-phase interview study of why and how university students hand academic messages to AI. With 12 participants across North American universities we ran a digital-whiteboard elicitation and in-depth walkthroughs, producing 110 recipient-channel groups consolidated into 31 recurring contexts, then built a typology of AI-use orientations through sentence-level open coding. AI use was highest for institutional recipients reached over email and the LMS and lowest for peers on social media. We are extending the sample to 17 for a full manuscript.

  • Jared Ren and Soobin Cho. Delegating Before Learning. CSCW 2026 workshop Broader Impacts of GenAI in Communication. arXiv

Understanding Interns’ Experience of AI-Use Uncertainty and Its Effects on Well-being [Ongoing]

Lead Researcher, University of Washington, with Colin LeFevre (UC Irvine) · Sep 2026 – present

A critical incident technique interview study with student interns in judgment-intensive roles: what considerations they weigh when deciding whether and how to use AI for a work task, what tactics they use when norms, permissions, or disclosure expectations are uncertain, and what well-being effects they name. Pilot and early findings are being prepared as a CHI 2027 poster paper, with a full paper to follow.

Human-AI Interaction in Creative and Professional Work

Professional Visual Artists’ Use of Generative Agents for Creative Work

Lead Researcher, University of Washington (4-person team) · Jan 2026 – Apr 2026

Interviews with five professional visual artists across painting, performance, commercial VFX, and editorial illustration on how they keep creative identity, authorship, and agency as generative AI enters their workflow. Thematic analysis in ATLAS.ti with a 68-code codebook; the core contribution distinguishes proactive, principle-based limits on where AI may enter the process from reactive, quality-based fixes.

  • Jared Ren, Manasvi Kale, Hsiang-Yi Lung, and So Eun Choi. “I Can Talk to AI, but the Making Is Mine.” HAI ’26, Osaka. To appear.

TraceQual: An AI-Disclosure Tool for Qualitative Analysis

Independent project · Apr 2026 – Jun 2026 · github.com/jaredren/tracequal

A Python tool on the Anthropic Messages API that reads a researcher-AI chat transcript from a qualitative coding session and produces a structured decision matrix: one row per analytic decision, recording what the AI proposed, what the researcher did with it, and how confident that record is. Versioned schema grounded in the qualitative-methods literature, a rule-derived confidence model, a four-state validation harness, and a Streamlit deployment path. Meant for a methods appendix or audit trail; it never touches participant data.

An LLM-Powered Dispute-Classification Tool for Wikipedia Moderators

Directed Research Group, University of Washington · Jan 2026 – Apr 2026

A prototype to help moderators identify each talk-page dispute’s main topic and applicable policy. We parsed and hand-labeled 170+ talk-page discussions to the relevant Wikipedia policy, essay, or guideline, validated inter-rater agreement, and fine-tuned a GPT-4.1 mini model in Python to predict topic and primary policy; delivered a working prototype for moderator testing.

Community-Based and Participatory Research

Participatory Mapping of Food-Access Program Awareness at Neighborhood Farmers Markets [Ongoing]

Research Volunteer, supervised by Dr. Elin Björling, University of Washington · 2026

Community-based participatory design with the Neighborhood Farmers Markets (U District). We co-designed a “map your route” activity and sticker cultural probes to surface how market-goers move through the market and how aware they are of food-access currency programs (EBT, SNAP Market Match, Fresh Bucks), and collected and analyzed 26 participant worksheets. The method is being scaled toward a participatory-design poster and a handoff blueprint that market staff can run themselves.
