I am a second-year M.S. student in Human Centered Design & Engineering (HCI) at the University of Washington, where I work with Soobin Cho on AI-mediated communication. Before Seattle I completed an Honours B.A. in Sociology and East Asian Studies at the University of Toronto, with an exchange year at the University of Tokyo, and worked as a UX researcher at the Canada Revenue Agency.

My research asks how people integrate novel computational tools into professional practice, and what they protect, delegate, or disclose as they do. I study this phenomenon-first and close to the ground: how students decide when to let AI help write a message to a professor, how professional artists keep authorship as generative agents enter their process, how interns resolve uncertainty about AI norms and disclosure at work, and how frontline public-sector workers fold new tools into their day. I work mainly through interviews, critical incident technique, and participatory, community-based methods, and I build small tools, such as TraceQual, that make AI's role in qualitative analysis auditable.

Research areas: Computer-Supported Cooperative Work (CSCW), Computer-Mediated Communication (CMC), AI-Mediated Communication, Human-AI Interaction, Future of Work.
